# Jeddah vegetation transitions and summer surface temperature: v8 follow-up 6

## Abstract

We compare Landsat-derived summer daytime land-surface temperature (LST) changes after NDVI-defined vegetation transitions in Jeddah with matched, never-vegetated controls. In the primary outside-built-up population, the outside 1–2-pixel own coefficient is about −1 °C/pixel across the full, far-ring-omitted and outer-ring-merged fits (−1.002, −1.036, −0.988); the selected isolated 1–2-pixel slopes are −1.136, −0.879 and −0.891 °C per own pixel at 90, 180 and 300 m. The 90 and 180 m isolation gaps remain after balancing observed baseline temperature, coast distance, built fraction, southern-belt membership and one-versus-two-pixel dose. The gaps attenuate in point estimates; the isolated slopes level off from 180 to 300 m. At 90 m the gap is larger in the southern belt, while isolated belt samples at longer distances are diagnostic only. External-pixel associations are model dependent and consistent with thermal-pixel blur or edge effects, but site selection and other mechanisms remain possible. The pooled −1.181 °C/pixel slope is a dose-weighted description of the sampled contiguous greening, with 55.7% of its leverage in one spatial block; it is not the marginal effect of a new small patch. The high-dose own-versus-external causal split is unidentified, and own dose does not vary at all among 9/9 cells. Irrigation identity remains unverified; water and energy quantities are hypothetical scenarios, not investment rankings. These LST contrasts do not measure air temperature, thermal comfort or population benefit.

## Design and population

The exported 90 m cell panel covers the intersection of the Landsat analysis rectangle, 39.0–39.4°E and 21.2–21.8°N, with the Saudi land polygon ([exporter](../code/gee/export_panel.py)). The four classes are: (1) greening, at least one of nine 30 m optical pixels transitioning from bare in both 2014 and 2015 to NDVI ≥0.30 in both 2024 and 2025, while all nine pixels were bare at baseline; (2) never-vegetated ring cells within 150 m of greening; (3) a random 10% sample of never-vegetated control cells more than 300 m from greening; and (4) a separate random 10% representative sample of other land, including existing vegetation, built and mixed cells. The matched donor pool uses class 3 only ([matching code](../code/src/fw_matching.py)). Figure 1a displays all four classes and highlights primary matched controls; the representative sample has its own pale colour. The dashed rectangle and land edge bound the map. Grid tile boundaries occur at 39.2°E and 21.7°N under the exporter’s 4×6 tiling, but the code alone cannot establish why any particular visible straight data edge occurs. Empty space can be outside the land rectangle or excluded by classification and sampling; it is not proof that land is absent. Figure 1b counts all 1,333 classified greened cells, not the 1,319 primary matched cells.

The primary pre-treatment-only matching retains 1,006 outside-built-up and 313 built-up treated cells. Figure 2 and the leading effect table use this population. Figure 3a pools 19,650 non-greened held-out cells from both settings for absolute-LST prediction; it does not validate a greening counterfactual. Figures 3b/c and 5 were produced on the narrower 744-outside/310-built-up concurrent-change match. They are sensitivity diagnostics placed in the supplement; their model-support counts and hypothetical resource ratios cannot be transferred to the primary population. In particular, support has **not** been computed for the 160 primary fully greened outside cells. [Population table](../tables/figure1_population_comparison_v8.csv) compares matched treated cells, weighted matched class-3 controls and the class-4 representative sample on baseline LST, built fraction and coast distance. All table and figure populations are indexed in [POPULATION_MAP_V8.md](POPULATION_MAP_V8.md).

The pooled zero-intercept slope weights a cell by the square of its own greened-pixel dose: 3.3%, 8.2%, 38.8% and 49.7% of its denominator come from the four classes. The largest spatial block alone has 55.7% of the total. The pooled estimate is −1.181 °C/pixel, its bootstrap median is −1.170, its 95% percentile interval is [−1.252, −0.925], and an exploratory block-jackknife interval is [−1.465, −0.896]. Deleting the dominant block leaves 641 treated cells and −1.042 °C/pixel. The class deletions above drop that same dominant block and its comparison cells. A percentile block bootstrap repeatedly samples a location carrying most of the dose leverage; its endpoints may be too narrow for transport to new sites. Neither bootstrap nor jackknife has established coverage here. Asymmetric intervals alone do not prove undercoverage. The 9/9 mean-cell contrast is not independently corroborated.

The 1–2 class exceeds a linear area-mixing benchmark by −1.188 °C in the primary match. The earlier −0.964 °C figure uses the narrower concurrent-change match; these are different populations, each scaled from its own 9/9 contrast ([primary mixing table](../tables/logic_primary_area_mixing.csv); [narrower table](../tables/followup_area_mixing.csv)). A native-30 m pixel-centred export finds only 26 of 426 classified outside small-patch cells passing an external-greening exclusion within 300 m of any own greened pixel. All 26 are primary matched, across 16 blocks, and their slope is −0.891 [−1.410, −0.425] versus −1.925 [−2.271, −1.477] °C/pixel for all 419 matched 1–2 cells; the paired change is +1.034 [+0.337, +1.642] °C/pixel. Positive here means **less negative cooling** in the selected isolated subset, not a positive temperature effect. Exactly one-pixel cells give −1.119 among 22 isolated versus −2.588 among 274 matched in the full class, a paired +1.469 °C/pixel. The isolated 1–2 cells start at mean LST 53.10 versus 53.94 °C and are 13.2 versus 16.4 km from the coast. The older 90 m cell-centre proxy gave −0.915 on 27 cells; a within-stratum cell-level isolated-minus-non-isolated LST-change contrast was +1.504 °C among 12 isolated cells. Those distinct distance definitions and estimands should not be merged. The isolation comparison does not distinguish neighbour contamination, site selection, or thermal retrieval artefacts. The sample does not identify spillover or sensor blur. For 3–4 and 5–8 pixels, only four and one outside matched cells pass the 300 m screen; none of the 9/9 cells passes, so high-dose misattribution cannot be estimated from the isolated subset ([dose table](../tables/pixel_isolation_by_dose_v8.csv)). The later saved v8d export counts unique external 30 m greened pixels at 90, 180 and 300 m. The joint-exposure coefficients and expanded isolation calibration are reported below as descriptive matched contrasts; they do not identify spillover or irrigation.

### Saved-data exposure and isolation follow-up


The [non-overlapping ring regression](../tables/ring_exposure_regression_v8_followup3.csv) fits own pixels and external pixels at 0–90, 90–180 and 180–300 m simultaneously to each primary matched cell's temperature-change contrast, with zero intercept and the unchanged spatial-block bootstrap. For all 1,006 outside cells, own and the three ring coefficients are -0.603 [-0.661, -0.439], -0.068 [-0.116, -0.053], -0.044 [-0.065, -0.030], and -0.005 [-0.042, -0.002] °C per counted pixel. Among 419 small-patch cells, the own coefficient is -1.002 [-1.172, -0.783]. In the full four-term fit, nearer external coefficients are more negative in point estimates; reduced models below test how strongly that ordering depends on the correlated rings. This association does not identify thermal-pixel blur or edge effects. External pixels can be counted in multiple focal cells, and the regressors inherit site selection. The southern-belt regression uses 636 cells in only eight blocks; the non-belt regression uses 370 cells in 26 blocks.

The three external-ring counts are not statistically independent despite their non-overlapping geometry. Among 1–2-pixel cells, their pairwise uncentred cosines are 0.832, 0.725, 0.897 (0–90 with 90–180, 0–90 with 180–300, then 90–180 with 180–300 m); the three-column normalized design condition number is 5.72 ([ring collinearity table](../tables/ring_pair_collinearity_v8_followup4.csv)). A leave-one-treated-block-out calculation tests sensitivity to individual blocks by refitting the matched control contrast after each deletion; it does not validate interval coverage. Across 32 deletions, the 1–2-pixel 0–90 m coefficient ranges from -0.099 to -0.069 °C/pixel, versus -0.084 with all blocks; 0 deletions reverse the sign. The largest absolute change is 0.015 °C/pixel. This checks one-block leverage for that coefficient, not the coverage of its bootstrap interval or confounding by several blocks. The belt has only eight blocks and several percentile intervals are asymmetric; an estimate close to an interval endpoint alone does not prove bootstrap bias or valid coverage ([all deletions](../tables/ring_leave_one_block_out_v8_followup4.csv); [summary](../tables/ring_leave_one_block_summary_v8_followup4.csv)).

A [paired reduced-model check](../tables/ring_reduced_coefficients_v8_followup5.csv) refits the same 419 outside 1–2-pixel cells and matched controls on the same 2,000 spatial-block draws. Removing the 180–300 m term changes the own coefficient from -1.002 to -1.036 and the 0–90 m coefficient from -0.084 to -0.073 °C/pixel. The remaining 90–180 m coefficient becomes -0.085, slightly more negative than 0–90 m: the monotone ring ordering is **not** stable to dropping the outermost term. Merging the two outer rings into one 90–300 m count gives own -0.988, 0–90 m -0.104, and merged outer -0.027 °C/pixel. The own changes are -0.033 and +0.015; the near-ring changes are +0.011 and -0.020 °C/pixel ([paired differences and intervals](../tables/ring_reduced_comparisons_v8_followup5.csv)). Thus own-pixel magnitude changes little, while the allocation of external association among correlated rings is model dependent. Neither the ring ordering nor a particular ring coefficient identifies a physical mechanism. Dropping the far ring changes the 90–180 m coefficient by -0.033 °C/pixel [-0.048, -0.013] on the same 2,000 block draws; this interval excludes zero. Thus its allocation is plainly specification sensitive, rather than a separately stable middle-ring effect. Across the three models, all estimated external terms remain negative and roughly 0.018–0.104 °C per counted pixel in magnitude, compared with about 1 °C per own pixel. These are conditional associations, not causal exchange rates. The merged 90–300 m model imposes one coefficient on two previously separate rings, so its decline cannot confirm the finer distance profile ([five paired rows](../tables/ring_reduced_comparisons_v8_followup6.csv)).

The [collinearity audit](../tables/joint_exposure_collinearity_v8_followup3.csv) adds the uncentred own/external cosine, condition number of the raw two-column design, normalized condition number, and zero-external share to every earlier joint regression. All 15 designs have algebraic rank two, but this does not identify separate causal own and neighbour effects. For all outside cells at 90 m, the uncentred cosine is 0.918 and the raw-design condition number is 11.0; for 9/9 cells they are 0.963 and 17.1. The 9/9 own count is fixed at nine, so its reported zero-intercept coefficient is a conditional level term, not an identified marginal own-pixel response. The high-dose causal own/external split is not identified.

The saved v8d export has only cumulative unique-external counts at 90, 180 and 300 m. It has no unique counts at 30 or 60 m, so 0–30, 30–60 and 60–90 m regressors cannot be recovered from this panel or approximated by cell-centre distances. The prepared but unrun [versioned exporter](../code/gee/export_external_pixel_subrings_v8_followup5.py) would use the same native-pixel grid and unique-pixel rule, check that the three subrings sum to the previous 90 m count in every cell, and report zero-count shares. A mostly-zero 0–30 m ring would have little identifying variation; no subring effect is estimated yet.

The 1–2-pixel isolated slopes are −1.136, −0.879 and −0.891 °C per own pixel at 90, 180 and 300 m. Their point estimates level off from 180 to 300 m rather than trace a monotone gradient; the smaller isolated samples limit power to distinguish the latter two distances. Within exactly-one-pixel cells, the unweighted non-isolated-minus-isolated gaps at 90, 180 and 300 m are -1.605, -1.660, -1.597 °C per own pixel. Their near-flat point pattern is not proof of zero difference across isolation distances: exactly-one isolated counts fall from 63 to 31 and 22 cells, and some exactly-two and belt strata have only 4–13 cells. It coexists with negative farther-ring coefficients because isolation and joint ring regression compare different selected populations and estimands. Neither supports a claim of zero association beyond 90 m. After expanded observed-covariate balancing, non-isolated minus isolated differences are -1.005 [-1.291, -0.462], -0.852 [-1.283, -0.186], and -0.691 [-1.184, +0.020] °C per own pixel. The point gaps attenuate with radius; the 300 m interval includes zero. The intervals condition on feasible calibration draws and do not prove bootstrap coverage. At 90 m the unweighted belt gap is -1.411 on 30 isolated and 176 non-isolated cells, versus -0.394 on 46 and 167 outside the belt. Thus the 90 m gap is larger in the belt; concentration across all radii is untested because only 13 and seven isolated belt cells remain at 180 and 300 m. [The subgroup table](../tables/isolation_subgroups_v8_followup3.csv) gives every belt/non-belt and exactly-one/exactly-two count and slope. Exactly-two isolated cells number 13, seven and four at 90, 180 and 300 m; all those rows are diagnostic only, as is any other row below 20 cells. Belt, pixel mix, land use and unmeasured differences remain possible explanations despite balancing selected observed moments. High-dose isolation comparisons are untestable.

The relative-spread convergence calculation has been moved to [the supplement](SUPPLEMENT_RELATIVE_SPREAD_V8_FOLLOWUP3.md). Its shrinking absolute class spread is partly mechanical because the combined-exposure denominator grows; it is not evidence of a physical convergence or a water ranking.

The common-month June–September sensitivity gives −1.212 °C/pixel on 971 outside primary matched cells versus −1.181 on 1,006 in the May–September export. Holding the old classification and comparisons fixed on 957 shared cells changes the slope by −0.005 [−0.019, +0.005] °C/pixel. The new panel has 1,273 versus 1,333 classified greened cells: 94 exits, 34 entries and 67 dose-class shifts. Five outside 9/9 exits and nine of ten 9/9-to-5–8 shifts lie in the southern belt. The shared-cell estimate argues against a large calendar effect on those cells, while the full-population shift is not a clean May effect or a general robustness claim ([composition](../tables/gee_common_month_composition_v6.csv); [matched comparison](../tables/gee_common_month_primary_v6.csv); [paired comparison](../tables/gee_common_month_paired_v6.csv)). The absent May 2014 scene means the full baseline comparison remains conditional on this export design.

Relaxing the stable-control surface rule changes the primary outside slope from −1.181 to −1.179 on the same 1,006 treated cells. The primary built-up slope changes from −0.783 to −0.713, so this rule matters more there ([control-selection table](../tables/followup_control_selection_primary.csv)). Matching only on pre-treatment strata avoids conditioning on concurrent development, but still assumes overlap and conditional parallel untreated trends. The late-greener placebo covers 375/1,006 primary outside treated cells in 2014–15 to 2018–19; early greeners and later drift are untested. Higher treated baseline LST could inflate apparent cooling through regression to the mean or differential untreated trends. The placebo cannot close that possibility.

## Limitations of the design

Independent or corrected LST retrieval is unavailable, so vegetation-dependent emissivity and thermal blur remain possible. Planting dates, irrigation records and untreated-trend histories are unavailable, so treatment timing and conditional parallel trends cannot be verified. A municipal allocation analysis needs beneficiary weights, comparable alternatives and actual water, wastewater, energy and financial limits; the present resource ledger has none of these and cannot identify a preferred investment. A practical Model A/B effect-error tolerance was not prespecified, so a support screen is not an accuracy pass. Irrigation identity remains unverified even where May–September NDVI stays high; groundwater, wadi and discharge-supported vegetation may persist in Jeddah’s dry season. The v8d export supplies unique external 30 m greened-pixel counts, but overlapping focal footprints and selected isolated sites prevent causal attribution of the external-pixel coefficient. The blinded imagery queue remains unlabelled; no land-use inference is drawn from it.
